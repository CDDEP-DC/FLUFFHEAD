    How this forecast is made

    Population and contacts. Each simulated region is a CSA or stand-alone metro area (small, strongly commuting-linked ones merged). GREASYPOP-CO builds its synthetic population from the 2015–19 ACS, PUMS, LODES and NCES school data: households, schools, workplaces and group quarters. Contacts within households are complete; workplaces and schools are stochastic block models (by income category and grade); group quarters are small-world graphs; community encounters are drawn from the residents and workers of a census tract.

    Transmission. Event-driven: when an agent is infected their whole course is drawn (latent, presymptomatic, symptomatic or asymptomatic, possible admission), and each contact gets candidate transmission events at the layer's rate. Community encounters are more frequent in denser tracts (rate ∝ density0.15, mean 1 within each region). Weekends, school calendars and a daily absolute-humidity factor (Shaman et al. 2010: ρ + (1−ρ)·exp(−180q), from ASOS station dew points, the NWS 7-day forecast, then climatology plus a historical year's anomaly per trajectory) thin those events; so do prior immunity and vaccination (CDC NIS-Flu coverage by state, age and month, 2025–26 standing in until 2026–27 data exist).

    Fitting. Each region is fitted to its share of its states' weekly admissions: its population share, reweighted each week by NSSP flu emergency department visits by health service area (states without sub-state ED data use population shares). The last two weeks are nowcast for backfill with flusight-fm's revision model. The effective reproduction number behind the latest data (prior 0.8–1.5, at the cutoff's contacts and immunity and the humidity of the two weeks ending a week before the cutoff; each region maps it to transmissibility with short seeded runs), prior immunity (a multiplier of 0.5–1.5 on 25%, 30%, 40%, 35% and 30% at ages 0–4, 5–17, 18–49, 50–64 and 65+), the vaccine's effect on infection (30–65%), the travel share of infection risk (1–4% for the average resident, one prior for all regions; applied to national incidence and spread by exposure: airport traffic, age, visitor-facing work tract, with boundary commuters adding a fixed share) and the humidity floor ρ (0.4–0.6, a winter rise in R of about ×1.2–1.3; data before November can't identify it) are fitted by adaptive importance sampling (three rounds, each proposing around the best draws so far) with a negative binomial likelihood that weights recent weeks more (4-week half-life), plus a term matching the model's growth rate over the last four weeks to the data's, so a run can't fit the level while growing too fast. After the cutoff, national import pressure follows each trajectory's own epidemic in a first pass; a second pass sums those forecasts into a national curve and re-runs every region's forecast with imports following it. Model admissions are the simulated epidemic times a per-state multiplier (log-normal prior, median 1) plus a constant background set from the season's first four weeks. If no draw follows the data closely the likelihood is tempered (power φ < 1) to keep at least 20 effective draws. Kept draws are continued to the end of the season several times each, and each state's part of a region is then rescaled so its trajectories start from the latest week's data (one factor for all trajectories, so their spread is kept).

    From regions to states. A state's forecast is the sum of its simulated parts divided by the share of the state they cover: the rest of the state is assumed to share their per-capita rate. Puerto Rico follows Miami's per-capita curve, levelled to its own recent reports. Regions are tied together by a Gaussian copula on their season totals, so the national total keeps realistic spread. US = sum of the jurisdictions.

    Known limits. Importation pressure after the data cutoff is held at its last level. Disease parameters are literature priors, not estimated here beyond the four fitted ones. GREASYPOP uses 2019 boundaries and populations, rescaled to current hub populations.
